# NRI investing - the checklist nobody hands you

Moving abroad does not pause your Indian investments. It changes
their legal status, and if you do not tell everyone involved, you
end up with accounts that are technically non-compliant and a
correction exercise that is far worse than doing it in order.

This is the sequence. The order genuinely matters - several steps
cannot be started until an earlier one has completed.

> **Author's note.** This is a scaffold built from the standard
> process. Replace the marked sections with your own first-hand
> account - the timings, the branch that refused a form, the

> of a guide like this, and it is the part no official page has.

---

## 0. Before you fly

Far easier to do while you are still resident:

- [ ] Update your mobile number to one you will keep, or at least
      keep the SIM alive for OTPs. **Almost everything downstream
      depends on receiving an OTP.**
- [ ] Note every institution you hold anything with. Banks, mutual
      funds, broker, insurance, EPF, PPF. You will need this list
      repeatedly and it is painful to reconstruct from abroad.
- [ ] Check whether your net banking works from a foreign IP.
- [ ] Consider granting a Power of Attorney to someone you trust.
      Several processes are far simpler with one.

---

## 1. Your residential status actually changes

Status is determined by day-count rules, not by intention or by
when you told anyone. Once you become non-resident, holding
resident-status accounts is not permitted, and the obligation to
tell the institution is yours.

- [ ] Determine the exact date your status changed.
- [ ] Note that the tax year and your travel dates rarely line up
      neatly. Work it out rather than assuming.

## 2. Convert your resident accounts

Your existing resident savings account must be redesignated. The
two account types you will end up with:

| | **NRE** | **NRO** |
|---|---|---|
| Funded from | Foreign earnings | Indian income - rent, dividends, pension |
| Repatriable | Freely | Within limits, with paperwork |
| Interest taxed in India | No | Yes, with tax deducted at source |

Most people need **both**. Foreign salary goes to NRE; anything
arising in India goes to NRO.

- [ ] Redesignate the existing resident account to NRO (this is
      usually a conversion, not a new account).
- [ ] Open an NRE account.
- [ ] Update the mandate and nomination on both.

*→ Your experience: which bank, what forms, how long, and what went
wrong.*

## 3. Attestation - the step that surprises everyone

Forms signed abroad usually need to be verified. Depending on the
bank and the country, that means one of:

- **Embassy or consulate attestation** of your signature and
  documents;
- **Apostille**, if the country is a Hague Convention signatory;
- **Attestation by a banker** in your country of residence, which
  some Indian banks accept and others do not.

**Ask which one your specific institution accepts before booking
anything.** They differ, the requirement is often not published,
and an embassy appointment is not something you want to repeat.

*→ Your experience: which route you used, the appointment lead
time, the cost, and what the embassy actually asked for.*

## 4. Update KYC everywhere

This is the step people skip, and it is the one that silently
blocks transactions later.

- [ ] Change your KYC status to non-resident with the KRA.
- [ ] Update your address - many institutions require the overseas
      address, not your old Indian one.
- [ ] Complete **FATCA and CRS declarations**, stating your tax
      residency. These are not optional and are exchanged between
      countries automatically.
- [ ] Update the tax identification number of your new country of
      residence.
- [ ] Update the bank account mapped to each investment, from the
      old resident account to your NRO or NRE account.

**The mapped bank account is the one that bites.** Redemptions
fail, or land in a closed account, if the fund house still has the
old one.

*→ Your experience: how many institutions, which ones were painful,
and how long each took.*

## 5. Check what you are still allowed to hold

- [ ] Some fund houses **do not accept investments from residents
      of certain countries**, most commonly the United States and
      Canada, because of local registration requirements. Check
      before assuming your existing SIP can continue.
- [ ] PPF and some small savings schemes have specific rules for
      non-residents. Find out what applies to yours rather than
      leaving it running.
- [ ] EPF has its own treatment. Decide deliberately whether to
      withdraw, transfer or leave it.

*→ Your experience: which houses accepted you, which did not.*

---

## 6. Restart the investing

Once the accounts and KYC are straight:

- [ ] Set up fresh mandates from the NRE or NRO account. Old
      resident mandates will not carry over.
- [ ] Decide which account funds which investment - this decides
      repatriability later, and it is far easier to get right now
      than to unpick.
- [ ] Re-establish the SIP dates against your new pay cycle.

---

## 7. Tax, in two countries at once

You may be taxable in both your country of residence and India.
Most pairs of countries have a treaty to avoid taxing the same
income twice, but claiming relief requires paperwork - commonly a
tax residency certificate from your new country.

- [ ] Find out whether a treaty applies to you.
- [ ] Find out what tax is deducted at source on your Indian
      income, and at what rate for a non-resident.
- [ ] Keep the paperwork. Relief is claimed, not granted
      automatically.

**This portal models Indian capital gains tax in full.** It does
not model your country of residence's treatment, or any treaty
between them. The tax regime selector says so where you choose it.
